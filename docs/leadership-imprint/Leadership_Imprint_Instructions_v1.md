# THE LEADERSHIP IMPRINT
## Pre-assessment instructions · v1.0
### Black Sheep Leadership Group

**Third build document.** Everything a person sees before question one.

⚠️ **These are not courtesy copy. They are a validity control.** Somebody who rushes, or answers as
the leader they would like to be, produces data that looks like a result and is not one. The
instructions are the only lever available on that.

---

## STRUCTURE

| Screen | Purpose | Skippable |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Before you start** | Conditions. Time, quiet, honesty | No |
| **2 · How to answer** | Mechanics and the forced-choice explanation | No |
| **3 · Who sees this** | Confidentiality. ⚠️ **Different copy per context** | No |
| **4 · Ready** | Final confirmation, then begin | No |

⚠️ **No screen is skippable and there is no "skip intro" link.** A person who skips the framing is
the person whose data you cannot use.

**Estimated read: 90 seconds across four screens.**

# SCREEN 1 · BEFORE YOU START

## Shared copy — both contexts

**BEFORE YOU START**

> This takes about **fifteen minutes**.
>
> It is worth doing properly, and it is worth doing once. Here is what makes the difference.

**GIVE IT A QUIET FIFTEEN MINUTES**

> **Do this in one sitting.** There is no way to save halfway through, so if you close the tab you
> will start again from the beginning.
>
> Not in a meeting. Not between two other things. Not on your phone while something else is
> happening.
>
> The questions are short but several of them will make you stop and think, and the thinking is
> where the accuracy comes from. If you rush it, you will get a result that describes somebody
> slightly like you.

**ANSWER AS YOU ACTUALLY ARE**

> This is the part that matters most.
>
> Everybody has a version of themselves they would prefer to be. Most of us have a fairly clear
> picture of the leader we are working toward becoming.
>
> **This assessment is not interested in that person.** It is interested in what you actually do on
> an ordinary Tuesday, including the parts you are not proud of.
>
> If you answer as the leader you are becoming, the report will describe them. And that report will
> be no use to you, because you are the one who has to work on Tuesday.

**THERE IS NO GOOD SCORE**

> There is no right answer in here and no style that comes out better than the others.
>
> Every one of the four leadership styles this measures produces excellent leaders and poor ones.
> The report will tell you what yours does well and what it costs, and both of those are true at
> the same time.
>
> **You are not being scored. You are being described.**

# SCREEN 2 · HOW TO ANSWER

## Shared copy — both contexts

**HOW TO ANSWER**

**GO WITH YOUR FIRST INSTINCT**

> Read the question, notice what comes to you, and answer that.
>
> If you find yourself building a case for one answer, that is a sign you have moved from
> *describing yourself* to *arguing for yourself*. Go back to the instinct.

**YOU WILL HAVE TO PICK ONE**

> Most of the questions give you two statements and ask which is **more** true of you. There is no
> middle option, and that is deliberate.
>
> When people are given a middle option, most of them take it, and the result is a report full of
> averages that describes nobody.
>
> **Both statements will often be true of you.** That is fine and it is expected. Pick the one that
> is more true, more often. Even a small lean is real information.

**"IT DEPENDS" IS NOT A PROBLEM**

> Several questions will make you think *well, it depends on the situation.* Of course it does.
> You are a leader and you adapt.
>
> Answer for what you do **most of the time, when nothing is forcing you either way.** That is your
> default, and your default is what this measures.
>
> The assessment asks about pressure separately, later on.

**SOME QUESTIONS WILL BE UNCOMFORTABLE**

> Part of this asks how you behave when things are going badly. Those questions are less flattering
> than the earlier ones, on purpose.
>
> **The honest answer there is worth more than the whole of the rest of the assessment.** Most
> leadership gets judged on hard days, and a report that only describes your good days is a report
> about somebody who does not exist.

# SCREEN 3 · WHO SEES THIS

⚠️ **This is the screen with two versions.** A person who believes their manager will read the
results answers differently, and you will collect a set of profiles describing who people think
they should be.

**Get this screen wrong and the data is worthless.**

---

## VERSION A — SELF-SERVE

**WHO SEES THIS**

> **You do.**
>
> Your answers and your report are yours. Nobody else receives a copy, and nobody is notified that
> you took it.
>
> If you choose to share your report later, that is entirely your decision.

**WHAT WE KEEP**

> We keep your responses so we can improve the instrument over time. That data is looked at in
> aggregate, alongside everybody else's, and never individually attributed.
>
> **What we do not do:** we do not sell your data, we do not share it with employers, and we do not
> use it for anything other than making the assessment better.

**[BUTTON]** `Continue`

---

## VERSION B — ORGANISATIONAL PILOT

⚠️ **Only use this version when the statements in it are actually true.** If the organisation will
see individual results, say so plainly here. **A false confidentiality promise is worse than no
promise**, because it produces bad data and then breaks trust when somebody finds out.

**WHO SEES THIS**

> **Your individual report is yours.**
>
> Your manager will not receive it. It will not go in your file. It will not be used in any
> performance, promotion or selection decision, now or later.
>
> You will receive your own report and you decide what you do with it.

**WHAT YOUR ORGANISATION SEES**

> They see the **shape of the group**, not the individuals in it.
>
> That means something like *"this cohort is heavily task-first and thin on measured pace."* It is
> a picture of the room, and it is used to make the sessions more useful.
>
> **Nobody's name is attached to anything the organisation sees.**

**WHY THIS MATTERS FOR YOUR ANSWERS**

> We are telling you this before you start for a specific reason.
>
> If you thought your manager was going to read this, you would answer differently. Everybody
> would. And the assessment would produce a room full of profiles describing who people think they
> ought to be, which would be no use to anybody including you.
>
> **This is not a test and there is no version of your result that reflects badly on you.**

**[BUTTON]** `Continue`

# IN-ASSESSMENT COPY

## Block transitions

⚠️ **Short. One or two lines. These are signposts, not lessons.**

---

**Before Block A** *(natural pace)*

> **First, how you move.**
>
> Two statements each time. Pick the one more true of you.

---

**Before Block B** *(natural priority)*

> **Now, what you protect.**
>
> Same format. Both will often be true. Pick the stronger lean.

---

**Before Block C** *(pressure scenarios)*

> **Now the harder part.**
>
> These are situations rather than statements, and they ask what you do when things are going
> badly.
>
> **Answer honestly rather than well.** This section is where the useful part of your report comes
> from.

---

**Before Block D** *(validity)*

> **Nearly done.**
>
> A few questions about how consistent you are. Rate how often each one is true.

---

**Before Block E** *(conflict — withheld)*

> **Last three.**
>
> These are about disagreement. **Your answers here are held back from your report** and released
> separately, because they land better with somebody to talk them through.

---

## Progress indicator

Show **questions remaining**, not percentage. *"12 of 40"* is more motivating than a bar at 30%.

⚠️ **No time estimate during the assessment.** A countdown encourages speed and speed is the
enemy here.

---

## Abandonment

⚠️ **There is no partial save and no resume.** If somebody leaves, they start fresh.

**Partial responses are still written to storage** even though no report is produced. Abandonment
position is diagnostic — if people consistently stop at question 23, question 23 is a problem.

**No "welcome back" screen exists.** It cannot happen.

# COPY RULES

⚠️ **Binding across every screen in this document.**

- **No em dashes**
- Grade 7 to 8
- Second person throughout
- Contractions fine and preferred
- **Never call it a test.** It is an assessment, and the word test invites people to pass it
- **Never imply a good result exists**
- Short paragraphs. Nobody reads a wall of instructions

## Words to avoid

| Do not use | Because |
|---|---|
| Test | Invites passing |
| Score | Implies high is better |
| Pass, fail, rank | Same |
| Personality | This is behaviour, not personality |
| Diagnose | Clinical, and overclaims |
| Accurate | Overclaims for a self-report instrument |

## Words that carry weight

**Honest · actually · default · costs · describes · fifteen quiet minutes**

# WHY EACH INSTRUCTION EXISTS

**For internal reference and for the technical manual.**

| Instruction | What it controls |
|---|---|
| Fifteen quiet minutes | Rushing is the primary cause of low-confidence results |
| Answer as you are, not as you are becoming | Social desirability bias, the largest threat to self-report validity |
| First instinct | Reduces over-reasoning, which flattens genuine leans |
| No middle option, explained | Prevents the midpoint clustering that ruins most self-report instruments |
| "It depends" addressed directly | Otherwise respondents answer for their best day, not their default |
| Pressure questions flagged as uncomfortable | Pre-empts defensive answering in the block that matters most |
| Who sees this, stated plainly | The single largest threat in an organisational context |
| No time estimate during | A countdown encourages speed |
| Abandonment defaults to restart | Split sessions produce inconsistent results |

⚠️ **Every one of these is a documented threat to self-report validity.** They belong in the
technical manual as evidence of instrument design quality, not just as user-facing copy.

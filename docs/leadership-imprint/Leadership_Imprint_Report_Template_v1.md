# PAGE 3 · ABOUT THIS REPORT

**WHAT THIS MEASURES**

> This report describes **how you lead**, not who you are.
>
> It measures two things. How quickly you move from deciding to doing. And which one you protect
> first when the work and the people pull in different directions.
>
> Then it measures what happens to both of those when the pressure comes on, because most leadership
> gets judged on hard days rather than easy ones.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEASURE**

> **It does not measure how good you are.** Every style produces excellent leaders and poor ones.
>
> **It does not measure your values, your motivation or your emotional intelligence.** Those are
> different things and other instruments measure them.
>
> **It does not predict how you will perform.** It describes how you behave.
>
> **It is not a hiring tool.** This is a self-report instrument. People can answer it the way they
> would like to be, and self-report instruments should never be used to make selection decisions
> about anybody.

**HOW TO READ IT**

> **There is no right answer in here.** No style is better. No position is better. No range is
> better. Each one has something it does well and something it costs.
>
> **This is a starting point, not a box.** You are more than four boxes and one of them will not
> contain you. What it will do is name a pattern you probably already half knew about, and give you
> something specific to do about it.
>
> **You will recognise yourself in more than one.** That is normal and it is why the report includes
> all four in full.

**[CONDITIONAL — low confidence flag]**
> ⚠️ **A note on your results.** Your answers were unusually consistent in a way that suggests you
> may have been answering quickly, or answering as you would like to be rather than as you are.
> The result below may not be an accurate picture. It is worth taking this again when you have
> twenty quiet minutes.

# PAGES 4–5 · THE FRAMEWORK

**TWO THINGS, MEASURED**

**PACE — how quickly you move from deciding to doing**

> This is not how fast you think. It is how fast you **commit**.
>
> Some leaders want the whole picture before they move. Others move on the picture available and
> adjust as they go. Both get there. They get there differently, and they fail differently.

**[VISUAL] Continuum bar.** `MEASURED ←→ FAST`. Neither pole styled as preferable.

**PRIORITY — what you protect first when two things compete**

> This is not whether you care about people. Everyone does.
>
> This is which one you protect when you genuinely cannot protect both. When the deadline and the
> person are pulling against each other, which way do you lean before you have thought about it?

**[VISUAL] Continuum bar.** `TASK ←→ PEOPLE`.

⚠️ **Fixed copy, must appear:**
> Task-first does not mean cold. People-first does not mean soft. Some of the most caring leaders
> you will ever meet are task-first, and some of the toughest are people-first.

**FOUR STYLES**

**[VISUAL] The grid** with all four quadrants labelled and the four hero images at thumbnail size.

**WHY A FLEET NEEDS ALL FOUR**

> Here is the part most assessments leave out.
>
> **Nobody argues that a tractor should be a rocket.** They do different jobs. A fleet with only one
> kind of vehicle in it is not a strong fleet, however good that vehicle is.
>
> The same is true of a team. Too many of one style does not make a team stronger. It makes it fail
> in a specific, predictable way. And too few of another leaves a gap that somebody eventually pays
> for.
>
> That is what the Fleet section of this report is for, and it is the part worth acting on.

# PAGE 15 · YOUR RANGE

**[VISUAL] Range bar**, four bands, marker at current position.

> **{range_band}. You travel {range_distance} points.**

**Full paragraphs:**

**Anchored (0–15)**
> You are almost the same person under pressure as you are on an ordinary day. There is real value
> in that. People always know what they are getting from you, and in a difficult moment that
> predictability is worth a lot.
>
> The cost is on the other side. When a situation calls for something that is not your natural
> approach, you may not have it available. **Consistency and rigidity look identical from the
> outside.** The difference is whether you could move if you chose to.

**Steady (16–30)**
> You shift a little under pressure without becoming somebody else. Your team would notice a change
> in you but would still recognise you, which is a comfortable place to lead from.
>
> This is the range most leaders would choose if they could. There is not much to fix here. The
> thing worth watching is whether the shift is deliberate or automatic.

**Adaptive (31–50)**
> You genuinely change when the pressure comes on. That is a real capability, and it means you have
> more than one way to lead available to you.
>
> The cost is predictability. **Your team may be managing two versions of you without knowing which
> one they are getting.** The fix is not to change less. It is to say it out loud: "when this gets
> tight, I go quiet, and that is not about you."

**Wide (51+)**
> You become a noticeably different leader under pressure. Not slightly. Noticeably.
>
> The capability is real. You have access to responses that a more anchored leader simply does not,
> and in a genuinely varied environment that is an advantage.
>
> **The cost is that people cannot anticipate you**, and under pressure they most need to. A team
> that does not know which version is coming spends energy watching you instead of doing the work.
>
> This is worth naming to your team directly. Not apologising for. Naming.

⚠️ **Fixed copy, must appear:**
> **No range is better than another.** A wide range is not more skilled and an anchored range is not
> more reliable. They are different, and each one costs something.

### The twelve pairings

**ROCKET leading a TRACTOR**
- *Needs:* Context, and time between the context and the decision. They are not being difficult when they ask questions. They are trying to do it properly.
- *Gets wrong:* You bring them a decision that needs making now. They freeze, or they agree and quietly do not do it. You read that as resistance. It was ambush.
- *One adjustment:* Give them the context Monday and the decision Wednesday.

**ROCKET leading a BUS**
- *Needs:* Two minutes of human contact before the ask. Not a courtesy with them. It is the thing that makes the ask land.
- *Gets wrong:* You lead with the task every time. They read your neutrality as displeasure and start managing a relationship problem that does not exist.
- *One adjustment:* Ask how they are before you ask what you called about. Mean it.

**ROCKET leading a JET**
- *Needs:* To know which priority is genuinely first.
- *Gets wrong:* You give them three things and assume they will work out the order. They optimise for whoever asked last, and you find out later.
- *One adjustment:* Say which one is first. Out loud, in those words.

**TRACTOR leading a ROCKET**
- *Needs:* The answer first, reasoning underneath.
- *Gets wrong:* You build to the conclusion. They stopped listening in the first thirty seconds and made their own decision about what you wanted.
- *One adjustment:* Lead with the ask. Put your working below it, where they can find it if they want it.

**TRACTOR leading a BUS**
- *Needs:* To know what you are thinking while you are thinking it.
- *Gets wrong:* You go quiet while you work something through. They fill the silence with a worse story than the truth.
- *One adjustment:* Narrate the middle. "Still working on it, nothing is wrong, I will have it Thursday."

**TRACTOR leading a JET**
- *Needs:* The destination, not the route.
- *Gets wrong:* You specify the method as well as the outcome. They experience that as being tied down for no reason, and they will go around it.
- *One adjustment:* Tell them where you need to get to and let them find the way.

**BUS leading a ROCKET**
- *Needs:* The ask in the first sentence.
- *Gets wrong:* You warm up first. They are waiting for the point and getting impatient, which is not about you.
- *One adjustment:* Ask first. You can be warm in sentence two.

**BUS leading a TRACTOR**
- *Needs:* Detail, written down.
- *Gets wrong:* You give them the gist in a conversation and think it landed. They cannot start without specifics and will not always tell you that.
- *One adjustment:* Follow the conversation with something written.

**BUS leading a JET**
- *Needs:* To be pinned down on what they actually committed to.
- *Gets wrong:* You take their enthusiasm as agreement. They meant it. They also meant three other things.
- *One adjustment:* End with "so what are you actually taking?" and write down what they say.

**JET leading a ROCKET**
- *Needs:* You to say it plainly.
- *Gets wrong:* You adapt your delivery to make it land softly. They hear hedging and assume you are not sure.
- *One adjustment:* Do not reframe it. Say the thing.

**JET leading a TRACTOR**
- *Needs:* One version of the plan, held still.
- *Gets wrong:* You adjust as you go, which is your strength. They cannot build on something that keeps moving and they stop investing in any version.
- *One adjustment:* Lock one part and tell them explicitly which part is locked.

**JET leading a BUS**
- *Needs:* To be asked before you commit them.
- *Gets wrong:* You say yes on their behalf because you could see it working. They find out afterwards and it costs you more trust than the favour was worth.
- *One adjustment:* Check first. Every time.

# PAGE 22 · LIMITS AND VALIDITY

⚠️ **Fixed copy. Candour is a credibility signal.**

**WHAT THIS INSTRUMENT IS**

> The Leadership Imprint is a behavioural self-report instrument. It asks you to describe how you
> behave and it reports a pattern based on your answers.

**WHAT THAT MEANS IN PRACTICE**

> **It is only as accurate as your answers were honest.** Everyone has a version of themselves they
> would prefer to be. If some of your answers leaned that way, the report leans that way too.
>
> **It describes tendency, not ceiling.** Being a Tractor does not mean you cannot move fast. It
> means moving fast is not where you go first.
>
> **It is a snapshot.** Your style is reasonably stable. Your range and your pressure move can
> change, particularly with deliberate work.
>
> **It should not be used to make decisions about anybody else.** Not hiring, not promotion, not
> performance. Self-report instruments are unsuitable for that and using one that way is unfair to
> the person on the other end.

**VALIDATION STATUS**

> The Leadership Imprint is behaviourally grounded and its items are mapped to defined constructs.
> **Statistical validation is in progress.** As the response base grows we will publish internal
> consistency, distribution and stability data.
